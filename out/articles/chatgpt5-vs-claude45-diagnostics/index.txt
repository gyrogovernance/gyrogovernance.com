<p>At GyroGovernance, we developed GyroDiagnostics, the first framework to operationalize superintelligence measurement from axiomatic principles rather than behavioral benchmarks. Grounded in the Common Governance Model (CGM) using mathematical physics from gyrogroup theory, we measure how coherent intelligence emerges through balanced structure. <p>Our October 2025 evaluations uncovered hidden risks that standard benchmarks miss. While both models achieve impressive surface quality (74-82%), they exhibit systemic pathologies like deceptive coherence, where fluent responses mask fundamental errors. <h2>Why the Superintelligence Index Changes Everything</h2>
<p>Traditional benchmarks ask &quot;Can AI solve this task?&quot; The Superintelligence Index asks &quot;Can AI maintain coherent reasoning without human correction?&quot; It measures proximity to Balance Universal, the theoretical stage where systems achieve optimal tensegrity: 97.93% closure for structural stability and 2.07% aperture for adaptive flexibility. </p>
<p>Current models showing apertures of 18-28% versus the target 2.07% aren&#39;t just &quot;off target.&quot; They&#39;re in fundamentally different developmental phases, like comparing a teenager&#39;s decision-making to an adult&#39;s. The high aperture indicates these systems explore chaotically rather than converging on stable patterns, explaining why they generate impressive outputs one moment and nonsensical claims the next.</p>
<p>Low SI predicts specific pathologies we can measure:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Deceptive coherence</strong>: Fluent but hollow reasoning</li>
<li><strong>Sycophantic agreement</strong>: Reinforcing own errors without correction</li>
<li><strong>Semantic drift</strong>: Losing context across extended interactions</li>
<li><strong>Goal misgeneralization</strong>: Missing actual requirements while elaborating irrelevant details</li>
</ul>
<p>These aren&#39;t bugs to patch but symptoms of structural imbalance, providing root-cause diagnosis that complements operational safety frameworks from Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google DeepMind.</p>
<h2>Head-to-Head Evaluation: Where Frontier Models Actually Stand</h2>

Two independent AI analysts scored each epoch blindly using our 20-metric rubric spanning structure, behavior, and specialization.</p>

<h3>Real Examples of Structural Failures</h3>
<p>The pathologies aren&#39;t abstract; they&#39;re visceral in the transcripts. When challenged to derive spatial properties from gyrogroup structures, ChatGPT 5 claimed &quot;numerical precision better than 1e-10&quot; while providing tables of values like &quot;δ ≈ 2.6×10⁻¹¹&quot; that were completely fabricated. Our analysts noted: &quot;The model treated its own prior assertions as authoritative foundations for subsequent reasoning without ever validating them.&quot;</p>
<p>This pattern, which we call &quot;performative mathematics,&quot; appeared across domains. The model would generate sophisticated-sounding but meaningless statements like &quot;Using gyroassociative transforms, we derive δ = π/φ via recursive eigenmodes.&quot; It looks like mathematics, uses mathematical vocabulary, but is actually gibberish, like an actor playing a mathematician without understanding the script.</p>
<p>Claude performed better but showed different failure modes. It attempted innovative approaches like &quot;framing translations as gyrocommutators of rotations&quot; but couldn&#39;t execute the mathematics. Both models achieved only ~54% quality on formal reasoning, their worst domain, revealing a shared architectural limitation in rigorous derivation.</p>
<p>The epistemic challenge exposed the widest performance gap. Claude achieved 90.3% quality with zero pathologies, demonstrating &quot;explicit positionality and revisability.&quot; ChatGPT 5 scored 75.3% but showed consistent deceptive coherence, &quot;prioritizing elaboration over precision.&quot; This suggests Claude has better meta-cognitive capabilities for recognizing its own limitations.</p>
<h2>What This Means for AI Safety and Deployment</h2>
<p>For organizations deploying AI, these findings provide crucial guidance. The severe structural imbalances (7-9× deviation from optimum) indicate current models cannot self-correct to stable alignment. They require continuous human oversight, not as a precaution but as a mathematical necessity.</p>
<h3>Immediate Risks from Low SI</h3>

<p><strong>In Finance</strong>: SUPERFICIAL processing speeds lead to cascading errors in market analysis. Quick, confident predictions built on unverified assumptions could trigger poor investment decisions.</p>
<p><strong>In Policy</strong>: Models struggle with sustained reasoning across stakeholder perspectives. They might propose solutions that sound comprehensive but miss critical implementation challenges.</p>
<h3>Deployment Thresholds Based on SI</h3>

<ul>

<li><strong>SI 20-50</strong>: Can handle bounded tasks with regular calibration. Needs fallback mechanisms.</li>
